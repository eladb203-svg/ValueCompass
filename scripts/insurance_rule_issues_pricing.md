1. **Q7 changed no quality score.** The Q4-flagged years (CB FY2023, RNR FY2023, AIG FY2024) now leave the cost-of-float series, but each company still has at least 3 valid years in its 5-year window and all remaining years sit below the yield, so 3.5.1, 3.5.2 and 3.5.4 are unchanged. The expected AIG FY2023 drop did **not** happen: AIG FY2023 is not a Q4-flagged year (the Corebridge deconsolidation is flagged in FY2024). In FY2023 the underwriting result excludes Corebridge (restated as discontinued operations) while the average float (FY2022 271.8bn, FY2023 222.2bn) still includes it, so the FY2023 cost of float (-3.3%) mixes a numerator and a denominator with different perimeters. Decision needed: add an FY2023 event row for AIG (Corebridge held for sale / deconsolidation path) or exclude years where float and underwriting result have different perimeters.
2. **Data fix inside the pricing path (WRB).** WRB's diluted share counts for FY2017-FY2022 are tagged in thousands in SEC data (about 435,000 instead of 435 million), which made the operating EPS about 1,000 times too large and the Graham Number 738. `operating_eps_series` now rescales a count that is about 1,000 times smaller than the period-end count and flags each rescaled year; a larger mismatch makes the year missing. The ETL and the other models are untouched. Decision needed: fix the scale at the source (`sec_companyfacts` / `insurance_facts`) so reported-EPS-based items cannot be affected.
3. **HIG year series has a gap.** The extracted HIG series contains FY2015 and then FY2017 (FY2016 missing); the 10-year operating EPS average therefore spans 2015 and 2017-2025. It is used as is (10 years available); the FY2016 filing needs to be located in the ETL.
4. **Growth gate fails for 7 of 12 companies (ALL, CINF, RNR, MET, PRU, AFL, AIG)**, so g1 falls to the 2.5% terminal rate. The gate conditions (revenue rose in 3 of 4 years, EPS fell more than 10% in at most 1 year, EPS positive in all 5 years) were designed for banks; total revenue of insurers includes investment gains and losses, so "revenue rose" is noisy (PRU, AFL and AIG rose in only 1 of 4 transitions; not yet analysed by component). Value is conservative for AFL and PRU if this is noise. Decision needed: whether insurers need a premium-based revenue test.
5. **ROE_norm above 10% drives most of the value spread.** Nine companies have ROE_norm > 10% (CB, TRV, PGR, ALL, WRB, RNR, PRU, AFL, HIG), so the erosion sensitivity cuts value by 27-52% (PGR 145 -> 70, RNR 576 -> 332). The base value assumes the whole excess return fades only after 10 years; the user should read the range, not the point, for PGR, RNR, AFL, PRU and WRB. RNR's median ROE is 24.7% (capped at 20%) and is lifted by FY2023-FY2025 after the Validus acquisition; its operating EPS jumps from 7.3 to 55 in the same period, so a 5-year median is dominated by three post-acquisition years.
6. **Payout 100% (MET, PRU, AIG).** The payout ratio is capped at 100% of normalized earnings; with the gate failing g1 is 2.5% anyway, so this has no effect on value, but the "retention" arithmetic is degenerate for these three.
7. **Graham uses 10 years including loss years** (ALL FY2022-FY2023, RNR FY2017, AIG FY2020). The averages remain positive for all 12 companies, so all 12 Graham Numbers can be computed. The reported-EPS Graham Number is within 10% of the operating one for 9 companies; the largest gaps are CINF (142.5 reported vs 106.3 operating, +34%; reported EPS includes equity-security gains), AIG (60.8 vs 72.2, -16%) and RNR (292.0 vs 333.5, -12%).
8. **Price score hidden rule.** All 12 Graham qualities are at least 60.1 (the lowest is AIG; PRU is 62.1), so the "show the price score only if Graham quality >= 55" rule hides nothing in this run. Buffett buy prices are `not_suitable` for MET and AIG (Buffett quality below 55).
9. **Prices.** Latest close from the existing yfinance cache, dated 2026-10-12 for all 12 tickers; USD for all 12. Shares used are period-end shares outstanding from the latest annual report, not the current count, so buybacks since the fiscal year-end are not reflected in the per-share values.
