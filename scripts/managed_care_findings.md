**Data gap, not a model distortion:** CI stopped tagging purchases of property, plant and equipment with a standard tag after 2019, so capex is missing for FY2020-FY2025. Result: Buffett 3.5.1-3.5.3, 3.7.3, 3.7.4, 3.8.3 and Graham 3.5.4, 3.8.1 are missing, Buffett 3.5 and 3.7 and Graham 3.8 are invalid, and coverage is 72% (Buffett) / 88% (Graham). CI also has no standard tag for the claims liability, so its INSURANCE ratio (7.8%) is a lower bound.

**Items that look sound for this business model:** operating-margin stability (3.2.2), net-income and EPS consistency (3.3, Graham 3.4), debt/equity and interest coverage (Graham 3.3.1, 3.3.3), dilution and distribution items. Low dividends at CNC and MOH (Graham 3.5 = 0/10) reflect their dividend policy, not a structural distortion.

**Not captured by any item:** regulatory (statutory) capital adequacy of the insurance subsidiaries (e.g. CI reports statutory capital and surplus of 13.0bn); nothing in the STANDARD model measures it.

**Profile check remarks:** all six fail the INSURANCE test (highest MOH 42.5%, from the claims liability alone; the other plans 18.9%-36.2%; CI 7.8% is a lower bound). MOH is the closest to the 50% line; a different definition of insurance liabilities (for example adding medical costs payable to other claims-related accruals) could move it.
