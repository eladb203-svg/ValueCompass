Described, not fixed. "Literal" = what the code does. D1-D9 are the decisions recorded in the bank spec.

**A. Ambiguities in the spec (implemented literally)**

1. **Binding CET1 vs. the single required CET1 (Citigroup).** The binding ratio is the lower of Standardized and Advanced. Citi's lower ratio is Advanced (11.93%), but the supplied required CET1 (11.6%) is Citi's *Standardized* requirement (its Advanced requirement is 10.5%). Literal buffer = 11.93 − 11.6 = **0.33 pp** (Buffett 3.6 #1 = 0.5/4, Graham 3.2 #1 = 0.9/8). Approach-matched buffers would be 13.18 − 11.6 = 1.58 pp (Standardized) and 11.93 − 10.5 = 1.43 pp (Advanced). Citi itself calls the Standardized ratio its binding one. JPM has the same structure (Advanced 14.1% binding, supplied requirement 11.5% applies to both). The spec needs to say whether the required CET1 is approach-specific.
2. **NPL = nonaccrual + 90 days past due (and still accruing), as the spec says, is not comparable across banks.** The 90+ day accruing component includes government-guaranteed mortgages (WFC $1.7bn of $3.0bn; MTB $543mm of $561mm; TFC includes "Residential mortgage – government guaranteed") and credit cards (JPM, Citi do not put cards on nonaccrual: JPM $2.7bn, Citi $2.8bn of cards 90+ accruing). JPM and Citi do not publish one firm-wide 90+ accruing total: JPM = credit card + wholesale + government-insured mortgage lines; Citi = consumer only (corporate 90+ accruing not shown as a total). GS reports 90+ past due ($372mm) without saying how much is accruing, so GS NPL = nonaccrual only. BAC, FLG and FITB (nonaccrual) come from XBRL. The result is a mix of definitions (MTB NPL 1.31%, of which 0.40% is mostly guaranteed).
3. **ACL in "reserve coverage".** ACL is the allowance for *loans* (JPM $25.8bn, excluding lending-related commitments). The spec says "ACL" without separating commitments.
4. **LCR "not subject / not reported" (D9).** Five of 12 banks have no numeric LCR in the 10-K: PNC (a Category III bank, subject, but only says the LCR exceeded 100%), MTB (not subject; voluntary estimate 109%), KEY (not subject), FITB (no LCR disclosure; Category IV), FLG (never mentioned). All five are `not_applicable`, so a subject bank that simply does not publish its LCR (PNC) is treated like one that is exempt.
5. **Profile path 1 vs. 1b by the rule.** Citi fails path 1 on loans/assets (28.3% < 30%) and becomes **path 1b** by the "regulated BHC reporting CET1 and LCR" rule, in addition to GS (deposits/liabilities 29.8%). Both lose Graham 3.2 #3-#4 (deposit funding and stability). The user asked for GS = 1b only; Citi is assigned by the rule. JPM passes with loans/assets of 31.8%, just above the limit.
6. **FLG registrant.** The CIK 910073 registrant is now "Flagstar Bank, N.A." (SIC 6036, savings institution, not 6021/6022). SIC 6036 is accepted as "Banks" for path 1. The registrant is the bank itself after the holding-company merger; per-share data are adjusted for the July 2024 1-for-3 reverse split using yfinance.
7. **D3 loan mapping.** The spec says "bank's own classification" mapped to 5 categories in code. The mapping is my judgment per line: Citi's financial institutions / installment and other, GS's securities-based and other collateralized, and JPM's wholesale "other" go to "other" (50% of GS loans, 30% of JPM's), multifamily goes to CRE (FLG, 48% of loans), home equity goes to residential mortgage, credit-card/auto/personal go to consumer. HHI therefore depends on the mapping (GS 0.316 is driven by a single "other" bucket that is not one exposure).
8. **TCE per spec = equity − preferred − goodwill − intangibles.** Mortgage servicing rights and other servicing assets are not deducted (WFC $6.3bn, PNC $3.7bn, USB $3.2bn, JPM $9.2bn). PNC's XBRL preferred stock is $0 (par value only); the FY2025 amount ($5.8bn, incl. surplus) comes from the extraction, but earlier years stay at 0, so PNC's TBVPS/ROTCE history is overstated and its TBVPS trend is distorted (flagged `preferred_stock_latest_year_from_extraction`).
9. **Deposit funding (path 1).** The spec's denominator uses short-term borrowings; fed funds purchased / repos are separate balance-sheet lines (JPM $442bn, BAC $345bn) and are not included. Including them would lower JPM's deposit funding from about 84% to 73%. FHLB advances are only tagged for PNC and FLG (PNC's LongTermDebt may already include them: possible double count).
10. **Stress coverage (D1)** uses pretax PPNR (as reported, one-offs such as Apple Card provisions or FDIC special assessments not adjusted). JPM, BAC, Citi and GS reach the full-score threshold (median PPNR at least 1.0x of 3% of loans), so 4 of 12 banks score the maximum.
11. **Tangible value creation (D2)** keeps years with >10% share-count change, flagged. It uses declared DPS (or dividends paid / basic shares for Citi, MTB, FLG, PNC years where the tag is missing).
12. **Allowance erosion (D6).** The "worst year of 5" needs NCO, provision and pretax in the same year; WFC and TFC have NCO for only the latest year (XBRL history stops at 2021 / is gross only), so the sub-item is missing for them.

**B. Data limits**

13. **Charge-off history.** Graham 3.3 #1 needs 6+ years of NCO rate (10 for full points). WFC (2 years in the last 5, 7 overall), TFC (1 year) and GS (5 years) have too few: WFC is capped at 3 points, TFC and GS get "missing" (5 of 15 points), which makes Graham 3.3 **invalid** under M2 for GS (10/15 = 67%) and TFC. The NCO rate also needs period loans; WFC XBRL loans/ACL/NCO stop at 2021, TFC XBRL has gross write-offs only for 2023-25. Extracted latest-year values (loans, NCO, average loans) are overridden for 2025.
14. **Period-end shares.** The balance-sheet tag is missing for Citi, PNC, MTB, KEY (all years) and WFC (to 2021); the cover-page count (a few weeks after year-end) is used and flagged. Citi's dividend per share tag is missing (derived from dividends paid / basic shares).
15. **Intangibles** have no tag for JPM before FY2022 and PNC at all (assumed 0, flagged). Provision for credit losses is derived for BAC, PNC and MTB (loan + off-balance-sheet expense, or NII − NII after provision). Net income to the parent is derived for PNC and TFC (ProfitLoss − noncontrolling interest).
16. **M9 range vs. M3 score.** When a criterion is invalid (GS and TFC Graham 3.3), the score is computed on the valid criteria only (GS 80.5) while the min-max range still includes the invalid criterion with its observed points (68-77). The score can therefore lie outside its own range.
17. **Extraction method.** Quotes come from the converted 10-K text (tables flattened with `|`); amounts reported in billions (BAC RWA, Citi consumer loans, uninsured deposits) were converted to USD millions and are noted in the table. Uninsured deposits (display only) were found for JPM, BAC, C, WFC, PNC, MTB and KEY only; USB, TFC, FITB, FLG and GS did not state a total in the 10-K text (not extracted). Extraction was done by me from the text, not independently re-checked against Pillar 3 reports.

**C. Results that look economically odd (rule applied literally)**

18. **Capital buffer saturates.** Most banks have a buffer of 3.1-5.8 pp above the required CET1, i.e. above the full-score thresholds (Buffett ≥2.5 pp, Graham ≥3 pp), so the 12 points (4 + 8) barely separate banks except Citi (0.33 pp), BAC (1.4), WFC (2.1) and JPM (2.6).
19. **ROTCE cap and the price model.** JPM's 5-year median ROTCE is 21.0% and is capped at 20%, so its justified P/TBV is 2.33x against a market P/TBV of 3.11x (implied return 8.1%, implied ROTCE 25.9%). No bank has an implied return above 9.8% (USB): price scores are 0-2.2 for all 11 valued banks, consistent with the earlier US test rounds under the uniform 10% hurdle.
20. **Citi.** Quality 55.0 sits exactly on the Buffett buy-price cut-off ("not suitable below 55"), mostly because of the 0.33 pp literal buffer (issue 1) and a 7.9% median ROTCE.
21. **FLG.** Quality 24 with a -1.7% median ROTCE, NPL 4.9% (35% coverage), CRE/capital 386% (concentration 0) and an EPS-based valuation that cannot be computed (negative EPS). Its price (below tangible book, 0.65x) is therefore not rewarded by the model.
22. **GS (path 1b)** scores high on stress coverage and ROTCE because its loan book is small relative to revenue ($240bn loans against $58bn of revenue); the "3% of loans" stress scenario is not meaningful for an investment bank.
23. **AI items.** B3.1 is pending for every bank; B3.7 #3 (M&A gate) becomes a pending AI question for PNC, FITB and TFC (acquisitions above 10% of net income over 5 years).
